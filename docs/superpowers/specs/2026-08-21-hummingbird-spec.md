# The Hummingbird's Nectar Rounds — spec

*2026-08-21. Third of the three math animals. The owner's brief:
she needs to get FASTER at addition — fluency — without violating
the workshop's standing rule: no clock on a thing you cannot do yet.*

## The animal

The ruby-throated hummingbird: genuinely the Kentucky species,
genuinely the fastest thing in any garden — and RUBY-throated, for
the girl whose favorite gem is ruby. Speed lives in the bird so it
never has to live in a timer.

## The mechanic — speed felt, never shown

- A round is short: 10 facts from `math.add.fluency_within_20`
  (exists, 106 items), served as tap-to-answer with four choices.
- Each correct answer is one sip: the hummingbird zips to a flower
  and drinks. The round ends when the flowers are done, not when a
  clock says so.
- **No countdown, no stopwatch, no red anywhere.** Time is measured
  silently and compared only with HER OWN past rounds. The bird
  notices afterward, in its own voice: "that was your quickest
  garden yet" — an observation, never a demand. If a round was slow,
  the bird says nothing about time at all.
- Retrieval practice + spacing does the real work: one round a day
  earns the garden a new flower (visual, cosmetic, capped daily —
  the same one-wonder-a-day shape as the moth and the dig).

## Where it lives

At the bird feeder — a hummingbird feeder hung beside the seed
feeder (the real object; red nectar feeders are a Kentucky porch
staple). Tapping it starts a round. The bird itself is drawn hovering
— the only bird in the garden that can.

## The make-ten tip

Between rounds, one teach card: the make-ten move on the number-bond
flower she already knows (9+6 = 10+5). One strategy, shown not
drilled — fluency comes from the rounds; the tip gives the rounds a
method.

## Rules

Practice writes null-item attempt rows (`source: 'hummingbird'`) —
fifth subject on the pattern. No coins. Round length fixed at 10; no
way to chain rounds for reward (the flower is daily-capped, and the
second round of a day is just more practice, cheerfully allowed but
unpaid). Speed data stays private to the bird's own remarks — it
never appears as a number on screen.

## Out of scope

Subtraction fluency (same surface, later). Leaderboards of any kind,
forever.
