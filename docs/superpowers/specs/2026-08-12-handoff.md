## 2. The coin exploit — 499,926 coins out of a 590-penny shop

**What happened.** `sellGem` paid `valuePerGram` for ANY gem code, and the API
took the code straight from the request body with no check that she had ever
found one. A double tap, a retry, a flaky connection — anything replaying the
request minted money. `keepGem` was the same, which is where two diamonds in
her case came from. I had already written the correct guard once, on
`sell_kept`, and did not look at the path beside it.

**The fix** (`b5af1f5`): the server remembers what it handed her
(`currentFind`); keep and sell refuse anything that is neither in her hand nor
pending; both consume the find.

**The correction** (`c7e40d4`, `scripts/fix-coin-exploit.ts`): balance set to
330 — founding payment plus real digging. She keeps everything she bought in
good faith (log seat, lantern, both birdbaths), keeps one diamond, and the
second diamond was framed to her, truthfully, as paying for the art store she
asked for. She was told all of it, in a letter, as a mistake that was mine.

**The tail of it, fixed today** (`326f12d`, §7): the exploit fix left a refusal
path that was *silent*, and she found that too.

## 3. Two systemic bugs worth remembering past their fixes

**PostgREST's silent 1000-row cap** (`5de2fcf` `d10e7ea`). Four pages counted a
learner's whole attempt history from an unbounded select. Under 1000 lifetime
attempts, invisible; at Cecily's 2,061, every per-skill count on every map was
computed from an arbitrary window — and these counts unlock habitats. It locked
her out of the cavern she had already earned. Fixed with a paginated helper,
then properly with **migration 020**, a DB-side `skill_correct_counts()`
aggregate (330ms/2,400 rows → ~100ms/66 rows) with fallback if the migration
lags the deploy. The same bug class reappeared days later in the fact-accuracy
query — it produced a *plausible, wrong* ranking of her weakest facts. Any
unbounded select over per-child history is wrong here.

**`todayKey()` was UTC** (`516777e`). Louisville's day rolled over at 8pm
local, breaking every one-a-day rule in both directions — an evening dig
blocked the whole next day, and the same evening could double-dip after 8pm. It
governed the dig, the moth, and the review schedules. Now computed in the home
timezone, tested at both edges in summer and winter. Found because she wrote
"way is Crystal cavern not letting me dig today" and she was right.

## 4. The crash the whole pipeline missed, and what it bought

`f13be60`. A temporal dead zone — `cumulativeCorrect` read inside a `.some()`
callback eleven lines above its declaration. `tsc` clean, 1,321 tests green,
build compiled, **and her main garden page threw on every request.** TypeScript
cannot prove when a closure runs; `.some()` runs it immediately.

What it bought: `scripts/smoke-routes.mjs` — build, start, and walk every route
in a real browser, failing on an error boundary or uncaught exception. It takes
`--learner` because /garden was fine with no learner and broken with one. It
has already paid twice more: a hydration mismatch in Pip's tools, and it now
guards all 21 routes. **It needs `next start` running first** — it does not
launch the server itself.

## 7. Today's bug, in full: a refused sale must say so

The keep-or-sell modal showed **"sell it — 300p"** on a dug garnet. The server
correctly refused to cash a case gem — but as a silent `{ paid: 0 }` on a 200.
The screen played the happy harvest chime, said "Sold the Garnet for 0", and
closed. The stone was not sold, not in the case, and **gone from her view**,
because a dug find lived only in component state; the next dig overwrote it on
the server. The display case was worse: "sell one for 500,000" on her diamond,
`d.error` ignored entirely, drawer closed as if it worked.

Fixed (`326f12d`) with one rule: *a refusal is said out loud and the stone
stays visible.* Honest child-readable errors from the API on both sell paths; a
case gem's modal offers only "into the case" with a line about the Great Works;
the interior re-presents `cavern.currentFind` on load so a stone in her hand
survives anything; the display case only offers "sell one" on seam stones and
shows the server's reason instead of closing.

The pattern to carry forward: **anywhere a server refuses something a child
asked for, the refusal must reach her in words.** The dig cap already did this
well ("come back in four minutes"); the sell path did not, and the difference
was a letter from a confused seven-year-old.
