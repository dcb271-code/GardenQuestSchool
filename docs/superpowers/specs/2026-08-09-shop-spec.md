# The Shop — spec

*2026-08-09. Cecily has asked for this repeatedly and I have promised it
in writing three times.*

## What was promised

> "Coins buy things for the garden. Not seeds, because you already earn
> those. I mean things you BUILD and place where you want them: a
> bench, a stone lantern, a birdbath, a little bridge, a sundial. Your
> garden, arranged by you."

That is the contract. Anything this spec does has to keep it.

## The state today

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Cecily's balance | **1 penny** |
| Stones kept | one galena |
| Placement system | **does not exist** — the `decor` keys on some profiles are dead legacy nothing reads or writes |
| Seam stone sale value | 1–120p, average **28p** |
| Case gem sale value | 300p – 500,000p |

### The economy problem

The gem values were written to teach real relative worth, and they do
that well. As *currency* they are unusable at face value: a diamond is
500,000 pennies and a lump of coal is 1. Price the shop for coal money
and one ruby ends the game forever; price it for ruby money and she can
never buy anything.

**Resolution: two shelves in the shop, mirroring the two shelves in her
display case.** The values stay exactly as they are — they are true —
and the shop is built around them instead of against them.

## The shop

### The Yard — bought with seam money

Everyday things, priced so a few days of digging buys one. These are
what the promise was about.

| Item | Price | ≈ |
|---|---|---|
| Wooden bench | 40p | one Kentucky agate |
| Stone lantern | 90p | three agates |
| Birdbath | 150p | one pearl |
| Sundial | 220p | a good week |
| Log seat | 30p | |
| Stepping stones | 60p | |

### The Great Works — bought with a case gem

Rare, monumental, once-ever. Each is priced at almost exactly one case
gem, so the mapping is legible: *this stone buys that thing.* That is
the relative-value lesson arriving as a decision rather than a table.

| Item | Price | Buys with |
|---|---|---|
| Little stone bridge | 300p | a garnet |
| Glasshouse | 80,000p | a sapphire |
| Observatory | 200,000p | a ruby |
| Stone well | 500,000p | a diamond |

Four Great Works, and roughly one case gem every sixteen digs.

*Corrected after modelling it.* The first draft of this spec claimed
this "resolves over months" on 12% case odds at one dig a day — the
arithmetic says all four in about **1.2 months**, which is not what
"once ever" should feel like. Going to two digs a day would have made
it eighteen days. The dig odds are now 6% rather than 12%, so twice the
digging is twice the FUN and the same treasure per week. The real
governor is stronger still: selling her only ruby leaves a gap in the
case, so a child who wants a complete collection can spend duplicates
only.

### Treats — for the tunnel animals

Cheap (5–15p), consumable, and they pay in facts the way Luna's do.
One treat per animal per day, server-capped. Buying a treat for an
animal that has not moved in yet is not offered.

### Habitat upgrades

Functional rather than decorative — a better feeder, a pond plant.
Priced 60–400p. These want their own design pass and should be **phase
three**; the ornaments are the promise and should ship first.

*Explicitly NOT stocked: seeds or plants. I told her no in writing —
she earns those by practising, and selling them would undercut the
thing they are for.*

## The tension, and why it is the good part

Buying requires selling, and selling empties the display case she asked
me to build ten days ago. That conflict is not a bug to design away:
choosing between the ruby and the observatory is the most interesting
decision in the whole economy.

**It resolves through duplicates.** The case needs *one of each*.
Everything beyond the first of a kind is spendable. So a second agate
is money and the first one is a specimen, and she learns the difference
between a collection and a hoard without being told.

The shop should show this directly: when a sale would empty a case
slot, say so before confirming.

> *Selling your only fluorite. Your case will have a gap.*
> **[ sell anyway ]  [ keep it ]**

## Placement

Free placement, snapped to a grid.

- Tap a bought item in the inventory → it lifts → drag → drop.
- Snaps to a ~40-unit grid so it never looks accidental.
- Cannot land on a planting bed, a habitat, a path, or another item.
- Can be picked back up and moved, always. Nothing is ever stuck.
- No limit on how many items are out. It is her garden.

This is the hardest part to build well on a tablet and it is the part
that delivers "arranged by you". It should not be traded away for
speed.

## Anti-farming check

This world's oldest bug is a child farming easy content for rewards.
The shop must not reopen it.

| Coin source | Bounded by |
|---|---|
| Selling a dug stone | two digs per day, five minutes apart — **time** |
| Selling a mastery stone | one per skill, ever — **content** |

There is no third source. No coin is paid per correct answer, per
session, or per lesson. **The shop cannot be farmed because the money
cannot be farmed** — it inherits the caps already proven in the cavern,
and adds none of its own.

## The opening-day problem

She has one penny. A shop that opens to an empty wallet is a shop that
teaches her the feature is fake.

Two contributing facts:

1. Her daily dig gives ~28p average, so the cheapest bench is ~2 days
   away. Survivable.
2. **65 skills she has already mastered paid her nothing**, because the
   mastery award fires on a state *transition* and hers all happened
   before the award existed. Going forward she has perhaps 13 skills
   left to master at her current level. Her mastery income is thin
   through no fault of hers.

**Done.** A one-time founding payment of five stones, framed honestly
as back-pay — the cavern settling up for work she did before anyone was
counting. `scripts/founding-payment.ts`, idempotent, applied to Cecily:
agate, geode, quartz, galena and a freshwater pearl, **190p** if she
sells all five. They arrive as *pending*, so she still chooses keep or
sell on every one.

All five are deliberately from the seam shelf. The first draft spread
the rolls across the whole range and the last one came out an emerald —
150,000p, a Great Work handed over as a welcome gift.

## Data model

```ts
// world_state.garden.shop
interface ShopState {
  /** Item codes owned, whether placed or not. */
  owned: string[];
  /** Where each placed item sits. Absent = owned but in the shed. */
  placed: Record<string, { x: number; y: number }>;
  /** itemCode -> ISO date, for the one-treat-a-day cap. */
  treatsGiven: Record<string, string>;
}
```

Coins stay where they are, in `garden.cavern.coins`. The shop spends
from the cavern's purse rather than inventing a second currency.

## Phasing

1. **The Yard + placement.** The promise, and nothing else. Ships alone
   and is complete on its own.
2. **The Great Works + the case-gap warning.** The interesting economy.
3. **Treats.** Wants the tunnel animals bedded in first.
4. **Habitat upgrades.** Needs its own design pass; do not bundle it.

## Risks

- **Placement on a tablet is fiddly.** Drag-and-drop with a finger over
  a panning SVG map is the single hardest interaction in this app. Budget
  for it, and proof-render it at phone width before believing it works.
- **The garden map is already busy.** Twelve ornaments on top of beds,
  habitats and paths may read as clutter. The grid and the collision
  rules are what stop that; they are not optional polish.
- **Great Works are enormous and rare.** If a case gem is *only* ever
  spent on these, the display case loses its best pieces permanently.
  The duplicate rule is the mitigation and needs to actually hold — a
  child with exactly one ruby should feel the weight of that choice, not
  be tricked into it.
