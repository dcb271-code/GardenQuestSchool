## 1. What shipped

### Kid-to-kid mail, and letterboxes they paint
`184da4e` `aeb5d81`

The letterbox learned to carry mail between children. The write
screen gained recipient chips — the garden-builder, then each
sibling — faces over words, because the youngest writer cannot read.
A sibling letter lands on rose paper, raises the recipient's flag,
and carries a **write back** button that pre-addresses the answer. No
threads, no sent folder: a letter lives with its recipient and
writing back is a new letter, which is how real mail works.
`npm run letters` walks every box, so sibling mail appears in the
parent reading pass automatically and is excluded from
`--unanswered`.

Then each child got to **paint their own letterbox** — six paints,
six drawn emblems, free, no gates. The paint shows on the garden map,
on their letters screen, and on the recipient chips their siblings
see, so "which box is whose" is answered by looking. The flag stays
signal-red on every box: a raised flag is a message, not decor.

**Adoption is total.** Every profile has repainted: Cecily green with
a flower, Otto pink with a flower, Esme yellow with a bee, and both
shared profiles too. Nine days of mail carried a scribe offer, a
reform campaign ("stop making nonsense letters"), a peace offering,
a keyboard mash to Otto, cheerleading for their mother, a shout of
sixty exclamation points, and finally an unprompted apology.

### The Play Barn and the Munch Patch
`2926128` `7474629` `e8f5753`

Number Munchers, garden-raised, at the bike's **second road** — which
makes the art store's "more roads someday" retroactively true. A rule
crate, twenty veggies on a 5×4 bed, a bunny that hops where you tap,
and a groundhog who trundles predictable lines eating whatever he
crosses. What he steals regrows; the board is always completable.
**No timer, no lives, no way to lose** — the round ends exactly one
way: you cleared it.

The server is the referee: the board seed travels with the munches,
the API regrows the exact board and decides correctness, clears, and
the daily prize veggie itself. Wrong munches get a card **computed**
from the rule, never canned — the skip-count chain, the real sum,
the fraction explained in pieces.

Later, two crates for Cecily's untouched curriculum: **fractions that
are the same** (equivalence by recognition, with the flip 4/2 as a
trap) and **decimals: eat the bigger ones** (a longer decimal can be
smaller).

### The art store gets a door-knock
`89f0fca`

### The grown-up gate
`8df66b5`

The parent passcode cookie had existed for months and **nothing ever
read it**, while the child picker offered a big dashed "add a new
explorer" tile that went straight to the profile maker. A
seven-year-old used it to add two people to the family; one of them
silently swallowed a letter meant for her sister for a week.

Creating, renaming and **deleting** profiles now require the parent
cookie, enforced at the routes. Delete mattered most: a profile is a
child's whole garden, and any tab could have thrown away Cecily's
593 sessions with one request. The refusal is written for whoever
actually reads it — a child who found the button.

### A commissioned picture

## 2. Four things that were never reachable

This month's recurring discovery: features that shipped, passed
tests, and could not be used by anyone.

1. **Treats** (`34c60b1`). Cecily bought eleven over three weeks and
   fed nothing. Nothing was broken — all 26 of her animals were
   feedable the whole time, which is worse. Feeding was only
   discoverable by tapping an animal and noticing a button. Now a
   drawn bowl bobs over any animal she is carrying food for, using
   the same three conditions the button uses, so a bowl never lies.
   Her letter said it best: *"or show me what to do with it okay."*

2. **The ant hill, for Esme** (`66b7b3b`). Locked behind mastery of
   `math.counting.to_50` — a skill she has outgrown, so the planner
   will never serve it to her again. The door was locked with a key
   that no longer exists, and I had just promised her it would open.
   The curriculum itself resolved it: she has mastered
   `placevalue.tens_ones`, which *requires* counting to fifty. A
   prerequisite now counts as met if the learner mastered it **or
   mastered anything that transitively stands on it**. Only ever
   opens doors; every other child's gates held.

3. **The owl box** (`b0e5cea`). Shipped 2026-08-10 with a catalog
   entry, an interior, two species, a build quest and a researcher
   quest — and **no placement on any map**. The journal advertised
   it; the build route answered "no map placement for habitat". A
   month unbuildable by everyone. Cecily found it: *"The jrinal is
   saying that there is a owl box."* It now stands in the reading
   quadrant at (520, 240) and is drawn, not emoji-fallback.
   **Both girls built it within a day of the fix.**

4. **The letters CLI** (`d46dde8`). A botched argument order
   delivered the literal text `--to` to Cecily's raised flag. Caught
   by an adversarial spec review before she opened it; the script now
   refuses letter text that looks like a flag.

**The pattern worth carrying forward:** children take what the world
*shows* them and ignore what it merely *permits*. The Munch Patch
succeeded because the bunny asks. Esme built the owl box — visible,
drawn, new on her map — rather than the ant hill her sister
campaigned a month for, which is a menu action. Design the
invitation, not just the permission.

## 4. Rules added this month

- **Never emojis in new child-facing art. Always bespoke SVGs, and
  look at them twice** — once when drawn, once after any revision.
  Owner ruling, 2026-08-29. Existing emoji are grandfathered, not
  precedent. The second look has since caught: a crow clipped off a
  roof, a bite drawn as a sticker instead of a mask, heart-shaped
  radishes, a bee invisible on yellow, a title bar through a queen's
  chamber, and an owl box that was a mailbox on a post.
- **The child-language test now bans any bare "streak"**, not just
  "daily streak" (plumage survives — it matches the word, not the
  prefix).
- **Family management is grown-ups only**, enforced at the routes.
- **Refusals reach children in words** — extended to the habitat
  build route, which used to say "skill prereqs not met".
- **Check `error` on ad-hoc Supabase queries.** A select naming a
  non-existent column returns an empty set, not an error; this
  briefly looked like every child's habitats had been deleted.

## 6. How to work on this

Unchanged, and all of it still earns its keep:

- `npm run letters -- --unanswered` **every session.** Six real bugs
  arrived as letters before any test caught them; this month it was
  seven, eight, nine and ten.
- Run vitest, the server+smoke pass, and git as **separate
  commands** — chained ones hang (recovery: `pkill -f vitest`).
- `npm run build`, then `npx next start` and
  `SMOKE_BASE=… node scripts/smoke-routes.mjs` in ONE shell. Smoke
  caught a hydration crash this month that tests and tsc missed.
- Proof-render every new illustration and **look at it**, twice.
- Never fabricate a child's words. Test with the Friends profile and
  wipe the robot's traces afterwards.
